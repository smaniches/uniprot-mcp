    Sequence chemistry: scope, methods and checks¶

    uniprot_compute_properties and the "Sequence chemistry" section of uniprot_target_dossier derive seven properties from a UniProt sequence. This page says which sequence they use, how each property is computed, what the results were checked against, and where the estimates stop being reliable.

    Which sequence is used¶

    Both tools take the canonical sequence of the entry's FASTA record as it is. Nothing is removed. A signal peptide, a propeptide, a transit peptide or an initiator methionine annotated on the entry is part of the calculation, so for a secreted or otherwise processed protein the values describe the precursor and not the mature chain. Every result says so: sequence_scope in JSON, "Sequence basis" in Markdown.

    Human insulin shows the size of the difference. For P01308 the tools report 110 residues, 11,980.9 Da and a pI of 5.22. Those are the figures for preproinsulin. The entry annotates a signal peptide (residues 1 to 24), the B chain (25 to 54), the C peptide (57 to 87) and the A chain (90 to 110); mature insulin is the B and A chains only.

    uniprot_get_processing_features lists these boundaries for any entry. The server has no tool that computes properties for part of a sequence. To characterise a mature chain, take its residues from uniprot_get_sequence and use a tool that accepts a sequence.

    Two further points about the input:

    • Letters other than the 20 standard amino acids (U, O, B, Z, X) are counted under other in the composition and left out of every property, the length included.
    • ExPASy Compute pI/Mw, when it is given UniProtKB identifiers, computes for the mature chains and peptides of the entries or for a region the user selects (documentation), so the value it shows for an accession is not the value this server reports for a processed protein. To compare like with like, give both the same sequence.

    Methods¶

    Property Method
    Length Number of standard residues.
    Molecular weight Sum of average residue masses plus one water.
    Net charge at pH 7 Henderson-Hasselbalch sum over the amino terminus, the carboxyl terminus and the ionizable side chains, each treated as independent with the fixed pK listed below.
    Theoretical pI The pH at which that net charge is zero, found by bisection over pH 0 to 14 and reported to two decimals.
    GRAVY Mean Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy of the residues (Kyte and Doolittle 1982).
    Aromaticity Fraction of Phe, Trp and Tyr.
    Extinction coefficient at 280 nm 5500 per Trp plus 1490 per Tyr, in M⁻¹·cm⁻¹ (Pace et al. 1995). All cysteines are taken as reduced; the tools do not add the 125 per cystine of that formula.

    The pK set¶

    The theoretical pI and the net charge use the Bjellqvist pK set.

    Group pK
    Amino terminus 7.50. When the first residue is one of the following, its own value applies: Ala 7.59, Glu 7.70, Met 7.00, Pro 8.36, Ser 6.93, Thr 6.82, Val 7.44.
    Carboxyl terminus 3.55
    Asp side chain 4.05
    Glu side chain 4.45
    His side chain 5.98
    Cys side chain 9.00
    Tyr side chain 10.00
    Lys side chain 10.00
    Arg side chain 12.00

    The numbers are those tabulated in Biopython 1.88 (Bio.SeqUtils.IsoelectricPoint), which attributes them to Bjellqvist et al. 1993 and 1994. The table was taken from that source file; the two papers were not consulted for the individual values. ExPASy states that its Compute pI/Mw tool calculates the pI with pK values described by Bjellqvist et al., and that ProtParam calculates the pI as Compute pI/Mw does.

    Every result names the set and the terminal values applied to that sequence: charge_model in JSON, "pI and charge model" in Markdown.

    One rule differs from Biopython¶

    After a C-terminal Asp or Glu, Biopython 1.88 raises the pK of the carboxyl terminus from 3.55 to 4.55 or 4.75. ExPASy ProtParam and Compute pI/Mw both return 3.80 for GGGGGGGGGD and for GGGGGGGGDG, the same composition with the Asp one position earlier. Biopython, searched over the whole pH scale, returns 4.30 for the first and 3.80 for the second. This server keeps a single C-terminal pK and so follows the ExPASy result. On the proteins below the difference from Biopython is at most 0.05 pH and appears only for acidic proteins that end in Asp or Glu.

    Biopython's pi() searches pH 4.05 to 12 by default and returns the bound when the root lies outside. For prothymosin alpha it returns 4.05; searched over the whole scale (pi(7.0, 0.0, 14.0)) it returns 3.69. This server always searches pH 0 to 14.

    Agreement is established for these 51 sequences and no further. The ExPASy tools are web services, and their output can change.

    Limits of the estimates¶

    • Sequence only. Each ionizable group gets one fixed pK. The calculation knows nothing of folding, of bound ligands or metals, or of post-translational modification. Bjellqvist et al. 1994 report that N-terminal blocking in particular is a major obstacle to calculating a pI from sequence. Every cysteine is counted as a free thiol, including those that form disulfide bonds.
    • Where the pK set comes from. Bjellqvist et al. 1993 determined the focusing positions of polypeptides under denaturing conditions. ExPASy's documentation describes the pK values as defined from migration between pH 4.5 and 7.3 in immobilised pH gradient gels with 9.2 M and 9.8 M urea, cautions that predictions for highly basic proteins may not be adequate, and notes that small proteins with little buffer capacity are predicted less accurately.
    • Extinction coefficient. Pace et al. 1995 find the prediction quite reliable for proteins that contain Trp and less so for those that do not, and recommend measuring.
    • Precursor, not mature chain. See the first section.
    • Earlier values. A pI or net charge obtained from version 1.3.17 or earlier used a different pK table and is not comparable with current output. Molecular weight, GRAVY, aromaticity and the extinction coefficient are computed exactly as before.
